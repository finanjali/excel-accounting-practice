XLOOKUP / VLOOKUP

What it is. A lookup function. You give it a value; it finds that value in one column and returns the matching item from another column. XLOOKUP is the modern version (no column-counting, can search left or right, and has a built-in “if not found” option). VLOOKUP is the older equivalent.

Used forThe backbone of reconciliations and audits: matching check numbers issued vs. cleared, pulling a recorded amount for a transaction, and confirming during three-way matching that an invoice wasn’t already paid in a prior batch.
Municipal scenarioReconciling the city’s primary cash account against the bank statement — instantly matching the check numbers the city issued against the numbers that actually cleared, to surface outstanding checks. Also used to verify a vendor invoice number hasn’t already been paid in a previous weekly batch.
Syntax=XLOOKUP(lookup_value, lookup_array, return_array, [if_not_found])

Practice — easy → hard

=XLOOKUP("V103", A2:A5, B2:B5)

  2. lookup_array = A2:A5 — the column of IDs to search.
  3. return_array = B2:B5 — the column whose matching value you want back.
  4. XLOOKUP finds "V103" in row 4 and returns the name in the same row.

INDEX & MATCH

What it is. Two functions used together. MATCH finds the position (row or column number) of a value in a range. INDEX returns the cell at a given row and column position. Combined, they do flexible lookups in any direction — including two-way (row AND column) lookups XLOOKUP can’t do in a single step.

Used forBuilding dynamic ad-hoc budget reports where you look up an account code vertically and a fiscal month horizontally at the same time.
Municipal scenarioA department head wants a dashboard where they pick a month from a dropdown and instantly see that month’s figure for an account. INDEX/MATCH with two MATCH functions powers that intersection lookup.
Syntax=INDEX(return_range, MATCH(row_value, row_labels, 0), MATCH(col_value, col_labels, 0))

Practice — easy → hard

=INDEX(B2:B4, MATCH(5020, A2:A4, 0))

  2. INDEX(B2:B4, 2) returns the 2nd value in the YTD column.
  3. This replicates a simple lookup, but works even if the return column is to the LEFT of the lookup column.

SUMIFS

What it is. Adds up the numbers in a column, but only the rows that meet one or more conditions you specify. The sum range comes first, then pairs of (criteria range, criteria).

Used forYear-to-date budget-to-actual variance analysis — totalling expenditures for a specific fund, department and object code across thousands of transaction rows.
Municipal scenarioSumming all expenses where Fund = 100 (General Fund), Department = 4110 (Parks & Rec) and Object = 5020 (Operational Supplies) to compare against that line’s budget.
Syntax=SUMIFS(sum_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, [criteria_range2, criteria2], …)

COUNTIFS

What it is. Counts how many rows meet one or more conditions. Same structure as SUMIFS but it counts records instead of adding amounts.

Used forInternal control checks — counting invoices over an approval threshold, counting variances above a tolerance, and flagging duplicate submissions.
Municipal scenarioCounting how many pending invoices exceed the $25,000 threshold that requires City Council approval, or counting how many times an invoice number appears (a count above 1 means a duplicate).
Syntax=COUNTIFS(criteria_range1, criteria1, [criteria_range2, criteria2], …)

  3. Rows over 25,000: 30,000 / 45,000 / 26,000 / 28,000 / 45,000 = 5 invoices.

CONCAT / & Operator

What it is. Joins text from several cells into one string. The & operator concatenates two pieces at a time; CONCAT is the function form. Wrap a number in TEXT() to control its formatting (e.g. zero-padding) as you join.

Used forBuilding the long alphanumeric account keys that ERP systems require for uploads.
Municipal scenarioGovernment accounting software needs precise keys like 100-4110-5020. You string the chart-of-accounts columns together before uploading into OpenGov or Tyler INCODE.
Syntax=A2 & "-" & B2 & "-" & C2 or =CONCAT(A2, "-", B2, "-", C2)

Practice — easy → hard

EasyBuild the key Fund-Dept-Object (e.g. 100-4110-5020) for the first row.
FundDeptObjectFY
100411050202026
10041105202026
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=A2 & "-" & B2 & "-" & C2
  1. & glues pieces together; the "-" literals add the dashes the system expects.
  2. A2 & "-" → "100-", then & B2 → "100-4110", then & "-" & C2 → "100-4110-5020".
  3. Identical result with =CONCAT(A2,"-",B2,"-",C2).
Answer: 100-4110-5020
MediumBuild 'FY26-100-4110' from the first row using CONCAT.
FundDeptObjectFY
100411050202026
10041105202026
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=CONCAT("FY26-", A2, "-", B2)
  1. CONCAT takes a list of items and joins them in order.
  2. Literal text "FY26-" is included directly as the prefix.
  3. Returns FY26-100-4110.
Answer: FY26-100-4110
HardThe system needs the object code as 5 digits. Build '100-4110-00520' from the SECOND row (object 520).
FundDeptObjectFY
100411050202026
10041105202026
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=A3 & "-" & B3 & "-" & TEXT(C3, "00000")
  1. TEXT(C3, "00000") forces the number 520 to display as the 5-character string "00520".
  2. Without TEXT, the leading zeros would be dropped and the upload would reject the key.
  3. Join the padded code with & to the rest: 100-4110-00520.
Answer: 100-4110-00520

TRIM & CLEAN

What it is. TRIM removes extra spaces — leading, trailing, and double spaces between words (it leaves single spaces). CLEAN strips non-printing characters such as line breaks and tabs. They’re often nested together to scrub imported text.

Used forEliminating invisible formatting errors so lookups and SUMIFS don’t silently fail.
Municipal scenarioWhen non-finance staff submit budget data they often add trailing or double spaces ("Vendor Name " instead of "Vendor Name"). TRIM strips these so your XLOOKUP and SUMIFS keys match.
Syntax=TRIM(text) • =CLEAN(text) • =TRIM(CLEAN(text))

Practice — easy → hard

EasyA vendor name has trailing spaces, breaking lookups. Return the cleaned name.
Submitted Vendor Name
Acme Office Supply&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; (3 trailing spaces)
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=TRIM(A2)
  1. TRIM removes the spaces hanging off the end.
  2. The visible text looked identical, which is why the lookup was failing.
  3. Result is a clean "Acme Office Supply" that will now match the master list.
Answer: Acme Office Supply
MediumA name has leading, trailing AND double spaces. Return it cleaned to single spaces.
Submitted Vendor Name
␣␣Metro␣␣␣Water␣␣Works␣
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=TRIM(A2)
  1. TRIM handles all three problems in one pass.
  2. Internal runs of multiple spaces are collapsed to a single space.
  3. " Metro Water Works " becomes "Metro Water Works".
Answer: Metro Water Works
HardA value pasted from a web portal carries a hidden line break and a tab plus extra spaces. Fully clean it.
Submitted Vendor Name
␣␣Riverside␣␣Paving ⏎ ⇥ ␣␣ (line break + tab + spaces)
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=TRIM(CLEAN(A2))
  1. TRIM only removes spaces — it cannot remove a line break or tab.
  2. CLEAN runs first (innermost), stripping the non-printing line break and tab characters.
  3. TRIM then removes the leftover leading, double and trailing spaces.
  4. Result: a clean "Riverside Paving". This nested pattern is the standard scrub for imported text.
Answer: Riverside Paving
Interview prompt“Your lookup returns #N/A even though the vendor name looks identical in both sheets. What’s your troubleshooting process?” (Answer: suspect invisible spaces/characters — wrap both keys in TRIM/CLEAN.)

EOMONTH

What it is. Returns the last calendar day of a month, a chosen number of months before or after a date. The second argument is the month offset: 0 = this month, -1 = previous month, 1 = next month.

Used forSetting accurate month-end and year-end cutoff dates for accruals so liabilities land in the right period (the matching principle).
Municipal scenarioUsing =EOMONTH(StartDate, 0) to find the exact final day of an accounting period before the general ledger locks, supporting accruals and year-end ACFR workpapers.
Syntax=EOMONTH(start_date, months)

Practice — easy → hard

EasyReturn the period-end (last day of the month) for an invoice dated 2026-06-09.
Invoice Date
2026-06-09
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=EOMONTH(A2, 0)
  1. months = 0 means ‘the same month as the date’.
  2. EOMONTH returns the last calendar day of that month.
  3. June 2026 ends on the 30th.
Answer: 2026-06-30
MediumAn expense belongs to the month before it was entered (2026-07-15). Find that prior period-end.
Entry Date
2026-07-15
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=EOMONTH(A2, -1)
  1. months = -1 steps back one month from July.
  2. EOMONTH returns the last day of June 2026.
  3. Useful for posting an accrual into the correct prior period at cutoff.
Answer: 2026-06-30
HardTo prorate a monthly charge you need the number of days in the invoice’s month (Feb 2026). Compute it.
Invoice Date
2026-02-10
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=EOMONTH(A2, 0) - EOMONTH(A2, -1)
  1. EOMONTH(A2,0) = last day of Feb 2026 (the 28th).
  2. EOMONTH(A2,-1) = last day of Jan 2026 (the 31st).
  3. The difference between two month-end dates equals the number of days in February = 28.
  4. This day-count then becomes the denominator in a proration.
Answer: 28
Interview prompt“How do you calculate the correct cutoff date for prepaid expenses or year-end accruals to support ACFR workpapers?”

DAYS / YEARFRAC (with IF)

What it is. DAYS returns the whole number of days between two dates (end minus start). YEARFRAC returns the fraction of a year between two dates. Nested inside IF, they drive prorated adjustments and compliance timelines.

Used forCalculating prorated expenditures and the exact dollars to accrue when a contract starts mid-period — and evaluating use-tax timelines on out-of-state invoices.
Municipal scenarioA city contract begins October 15. You compute the exact days remaining in the fiscal year to post the correct prorated journal entry.
Syntax=DAYS(end_date, start_date) • =YEARFRAC(start, end, [basis])

Practice — easy → hard

EasyReturn the number of days from the Start Date to the End Date.
Start DateEnd Date
2026-06-092026-12-31
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=DAYS(B2, A2)
  1. DAYS subtracts the start from the end date.
  2. Order matters: end date first, start date second.
  3. From 2026-06-09 to 2026-12-31 is 205 days. (You can also just write =B2-A2.)
Answer: 205
MediumThe fiscal year ends 2027-06-30. A contract starts 2026-10-15. Find the days remaining in the fiscal year.
Contract StartFiscal Year End
2026-10-152027-06-30
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=DAYS(B2, A2)
  1. End date = fiscal year-end (2027-06-30), start = contract start (2026-10-15).
  2. DAYS returns 258 — the days of the contract that fall inside this fiscal year.
  3. That count is the basis for the current-year prorated share.
Answer: 258
HardA $24,000 annual contract starts 2026-10-15; the fiscal year ends 2027-06-30. Accrue the current-year share = annual × days-remaining ÷ 365. Return 0 if it starts after year-end. Round to cents.
Annual $Contract StartFiscal Year End
$24,0002026-10-152027-06-30
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=ROUND(IF(B2 > C2, 0, A2 * DAYS(C2, B2) / 365), 2)
  1. IF(B2 > C2, 0, …) guards the edge case: if the contract starts after the fiscal year ends, accrue nothing.
  2. Otherwise compute annual × days-in-fiscal-year ÷ 365: 24,000 × 258 ÷ 365.
  3. ROUND(…, 2) keeps it to whole cents for the journal entry.
  4. Result ≈ $16,964.38. (A 30/360 alternative: =A2 * YEARFRAC(B2, C2).)
Answer: $16,964.38

IFERROR

What it is. Wraps a formula so that, if it would return an error, it shows a clean value you choose instead. The first argument is the formula to try; the second is what to display on error.

Used forKeeping variance reports and public-facing transparency sheets free of divide-by-zero and lookup errors.
Municipal scenarioA percentage-variance formula (Actual−Budget)/Budget throws a divide-by-zero error for any department with a $0 budget. Wrapping it in IFERROR(…, 0) shows a clean 0% instead on a sheet headed to the Finance Director or a citizen portal.
Syntax=IFERROR(value, value_if_error)

Practice — easy → hard

EasyA department spent money on a $0 budget, so the variance % would divide by zero. Show 0 instead.
ActualBudget
$1,200$0
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula
=IFERROR((A2 - B2) / B2, 0)
  1. (A2 − B2)/B2 divides by a zero budget → a divide-by-zero error.
  2. IFERROR catches that error and substitutes your chosen value, 0.
  3. The report shows a clean 0% rather than a red error code.
Answer: 0 (clean, no error)
MediumA vendor isn’t in the master list, so the lookup errors. Return 'Not Found' instead.
VendorVendor ID
AcmeV101
MetroV102
RiversideV103
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula

  1. The XLOOKUP fails because "Zephyr LLC" isn’t in column A.
  2. IFERROR replaces the #N/A with a readable "Not Found".
  3. Cleaner than letting an error propagate into downstream formulas.
Answer: Not Found
HardOn the public dashboard, show the variance %, but if budget is 0 and there was spending, label it 'OVER (no budget)'.
ActualBudget
$800$0
Show formula & step-by-step solution
Formula

  1. The normal case computes the variance %.
  2. On a divide-by-zero, the second argument runs an IF instead of a flat value.
  3. If there was spending (A2 > 0) it labels "OVER (no budget)"; otherwise "On budget".
  4. This turns a meaningless error into an informative status for readers.
Answer: OVER (no budget)

Interview Questions

Two tracks: questions specific to municipal / governmental accounting (the Accountant I role), and general accounting questions you’ll meet in any staff-accountant interview.

Municipal / Government Accounting

  1. Walk me through how you reconcile the city’s main cash account to the bank statement each month. What are the typical reconciling items?
  2. What is fund accounting, and why do governments track separate funds (General, Water, Capital Projects) as distinct legal entities?
  3. Explain modified accrual vs. full accrual basis. Which governmental statements use each?
  4. What is an ACFR (Annual Comprehensive Financial Report) and what are its main sections?
  5. An invoice arrives in July for services delivered in June. How do you handle the year-end accrual and cutoff?
  6. A department is over budget on one object code. Walk me through your budget-to-actual variance analysis.
  7. What internal controls would you put around accounts payable to prevent duplicate or unauthorized payments?
  8. How do you ensure no invoice over the $25,000 council-approval threshold gets paid without approval?
  9. How do you prepare and validate data for an upload into OpenGov or Tyler INCODE? What commonly goes wrong?
  10. Explain encumbrance accounting and how a purchase order flows through the budget.
  11. How do you calculate and record a prorated expense for a contract that starts mid-month?
  12. What’s the difference between an expenditure and an expense in governmental accounting?
  13. How would you evaluate and accrue use tax on an out-of-state vendor invoice?
  14. Describe the month-end close steps you would own as an Accountant I.
  15. How do you make sure a public-facing transparency report is free of formula errors before it’s published?

General Accounting

  1. Walk me through the three financial statements and how they connect.
  2. What’s the difference between accounts payable and accrued liabilities?
  3. Explain the matching principle with a concrete example.
  4. Give the journal entry for prepaid insurance and explain how it unwinds over time.
  5. Which side — debit or credit — increases an expense? An asset? A liability? A revenue?
  6. How do you perform a three-way match in accounts payable, and why does it matter?
  7. What is a bank reconciliation, and what are the most common reconciling items?
  8. Tell me about a time you found and fixed an error in a reconciliation or close.
  9. What’s the difference between cash-basis and accrual-basis accounting?
  10. How do you prioritize your work during a tight month-end close?
  11. Which Excel functions do you rely on most, and how have you used them in accounting work?
  12. How would you detect duplicate invoices in a large transaction dataset?
  13. What is depreciation, and what methods are commonly used?
